# Citation keys used in scenario definitions

Each scenario's `literature_basis` field references these sources. They
establish its fault as a recognised class in the empirical literature rather
than an ad hoc choice.

| Key | Reference |
|---|---|
| `Zhou21` | X. Zhou, X. Peng, T. Xie, J. Sun, C. Ji, W. Li and D. Ding, "Fault Analysis and Debugging of Microservice Systems: Industrial Survey, Benchmark System, and Empirical Study," *IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering*, vol. 47, no. 2, pp. 243–260, 2021. doi:10.1109/TSE.2018.2887384 |
| `Gan19` | Y. Gan et al., "An Open-Source Benchmark Suite for Microservices and Their Hardware-Software Implications for Cloud & Edge Systems," *Proc. ASPLOS*, 2019. |
| `Chen25` | Y. Chen, M. Shetty, G. Somashekar et al., "AIOpsLab: A Holistic Framework to Evaluate AI Agents for Enabling Autonomous Clouds," *Proc. MLSys*, 2025. arXiv:2501.06706 |
| `Gunawi14` | H. S. Gunawi et al., "What Bugs Live in the Cloud? A Study of 3000+ Issues in Cloud Systems," *Proc. SoCC*, 2014. |
| `Silva20` | Silva et al., taxonomy of 117 microservice faults categorised by non-functional requirement. **Verify full citation before submission.** |

## Note on coverage

Zhou et al. is the anchor citation: it derives its taxonomy from an industrial
survey and replicates 22 real fault cases on a benchmark system, which makes it
the strongest available evidence that a given fault class occurs in practice
rather than only in the laboratory.

Verify every citation against the primary source before submission. `Silva20`
in particular is recorded from a secondary reference and its full details have
not yet been confirmed.
